SwarmLLMfirst, what a model is
No magic inside

An AI model is a very large book of numbers.

Qwen 3.8 is 27 billion numbers, learned during training. Stored compactly, that's a 15 GB file. It doesn't "know" anything else — all the behaviour is in those numbers.

analogyThink of 64 stations on a factory line. Each station is a table of numbers bolted to the floor. What moves down the belt is a single lump of dough: the model's current "thought."
27,320,000,000 parameters · Q4_0 · 4.5 bits each2 / 16

The hard part isn't the math. It's that each lap has to read all 15 GB of numbers. The speed of an AI model on your device is mostly the speed of your memory.

SwarmLLMbreakthrough 1
Nothing to install

It runs in a web page. Any device with a browser can join.

Browsers recently gained WebGPU — a way for a web page to use your graphics chip directly. It's the same chip that renders games, and it's very good at multiplying lots of numbers at once. That means: no app store, no drivers, no "works on Mac only". A link is the whole installer.

Open the link. The page asks your browser for the graphics chip.
Get dealt your stations. The room decides who holds what, sized to each device's memory.
Download only your slice — a phone might take 2 stations, a laptop 30. Cached for next time.
Start passing the baton. Ask a question; every device runs its part of every lap.

SwarmLLMbreakthrough 2
A from-scratch engine

~50 hand-written GPU programs, tuned to read memory the way the chip likes.

A GPU is thousands of slow workers doing the same thing at once. Speed comes from how they read memory, not how fast they multiply.

the 2.2× fixSlow: each worker fetches a whole warehouse aisle alone, walking back and forth. Fast: 64 workers line up shoulder to shoulder and sweep the aisle in one pass. Same numbers, 2.2× faster on the 27B.
one worker per row (before)
64 workers per row, side by side (after)
War story: the first "fast" version was 3× slower — one line kept four running totals in a tiny list, and the GPU silently moved that list to slow memory. Four named variables fixed it. GPU work is like that.
WGSL · 4-bit weights unpacked in registers · f32 accumulation · runs on Chrome, Safari and headless7 / 16
SwarmLLMbreakthrough 3
Hitting the ceiling

We measured the memory speed limit — and the engine is at it.

If every word reads 15 GB, the fastest possible speed is your memory bandwidth divided by 15 GB. We measured what the browser can actually pull through the GPU (184 GB/s) and what our engine achieves while generating (183 GB/s). There is nothing left to squeeze on this part — which tells us exactly where to look next.

SwarmLLMbreakthrough 4
Guess ahead

A small guesser proposes the next words. The real model checks them all at once.

The model ships with a tiny built-in "little brother" that predicts what comes next. Instead of one word per lap, we verify several guesses in one lap. A right guess is free speed. A wrong guess is discarded — the real model always has the final say, so the output is identical to not guessing.

guessThecapitalofFranceisLyon
checkThecapitalofFranceisLyon
writeThe capital of France isParis
85% of guesses land on everyday text, 97% on code. 9 → 16 words per second on the same machine, byte-for-byte the same answer. Across the internet it matters even more: every accepted guess is a whole lap you didn't have to wait for.
multi-token prediction · verified in one batched pass · exact rollback on a wrong guess9 / 16
SwarmLLMbreakthrough 5
When the room spans cities

Physics charges one ping per lap. So laps carry more.

Across the internet a lap costs a round trip — say 100 ms — no matter how fast the GPUs are. So the reading of your question sends 16 words per lap, and generation chains several guesses so a slow link still moves ~6 words per lap. The room measures what's actually fastest on your connection and adapts.

SwarmLLMthe honest slide
What it is and isn't

Private among friends. Not private among strangers.

what the design gives you

  • No counterparty. No account, no key, no server that could keep your conversation.
  • Your words stay on your device. Peers only ever receive the numeric baton.
  • Nothing to trust but your room. The same trust model as sharing a document link.

what it can't promise

  • The baton isn't encryption. Research shows mid-model numbers can be partly reversed into text — assume your room can read your prompt.
  • Long questions are slow to read. 14× behind native on prompt processing today.
  • A device leaving mid-answer stalls it. Recovery and spare copies are next.
SECURITY.md says exactly this, before anyone else has to13 / 16
SwarmLLMhow it was built
Small on purpose

5,400 lines. 49 GPU programs. 18 tests that check every output byte.

Every speed change had to produce the exact same words as before — tests compare byte-for-byte against a reference, and the guess-ahead path against the plain path. Speed without that rule is just a different model.

Measure before believing. A profiler that skips one kernel family at a time showed where the 111 ms per word actually went — and killed two plausible theories in one night.
Find the ceiling. A streaming-read probe gave the real bandwidth (184 GB/s, not the 273 on the box).
Compare to native on the same GGUF file, same GPU: 7.99 vs 9.0.
Keep receipts. A bench log with a row per change, including the neutral ones.
